---
title: The Funnel Formula
---
The Funnel Formula is nine layers, built in order. Each layer feeds the next, so a layer only starts when the one above it is locked. The first three are inputs, the fourth is the strategy decision, and the last five are the build.

## The nine layers
1. Client Context: who the business serves, what it sells, where it is today and the goal.
2. Brand Bullets: the voice, the story, the proof and the visual style, written as short bullets.
3. Offer Outline: the transformation, the timeline, what is inside, the price, the bonuses and the guarantee.
4. Strategy: which funnel, for which traffic, with which launch format.
5. Copy: every page written section by section with the 17Ps.
6. Design: the pages laid out around the copy.
7. Automations: the emails, texts, tags, pipeline stages and reminders that run after each step.
8. Traffic: where visitors come from, organic or paid, cold or warm.
9. CRO: measure each step, find the weakest one, fix it and test.

Copy comes before design because the words decide what each section has to show. Strategy comes before copy because the funnel type decides which pages exist at all.

## The strategy decision
Three inputs decide the strategy.
- Traffic temperature. Cold traffic has never heard of you and needs a lead funnel or a low-ticket offer first. Warm traffic knows you and can go straight to a sales page. Hot traffic is ready to buy and can land on the checkout.
- Price point. The higher the price, the more trust the funnel has to build before the ask.
- Launch format. Live means a set date, a deadline and a cart that opens and closes. Evergreen means always on, with a nurture sequence doing the work.

## Funnel type by price point
- Free: a lead funnel. Opt-in page, thank you page and a welcome email sequence.
- $1 to $100: a low-ticket funnel that pays for its own ads. Sales page, order form with an order bump, one or two upsells and a confirmation page.
- $100 to $1,000: an automated sales funnel. Sales page or VSL, order form and onboarding emails.
- $1,000 to $10,000: a webinar or VSL funnel. Registration, the presentation, the offer and a replay sequence.
- $10,000 and up: an application or book a call funnel. The page qualifies people and a conversation closes them.

## Live launch or evergreen
Start live when the offer is new. A live launch puts real people in front of the offer within days, so you hear the objections and the questions fast. Once the offer sells live and the message is clear, turn the best version into an evergreen funnel.

## Webinar or VSL
A live webinar shows you the objections in real time through the questions people ask. A VSL is the recorded and tightened version that runs every day. Many offers start as a webinar and become a VSL once the pitch converts.

## The funnel ecosystem
One funnel rarely carries a whole business. An ecosystem connects a lead funnel, a front end offer, upsells, a core offer and a high-ticket back end, with email and content moving people from one step to the next. Every piece has one job and one number that shows it works.

## Automations every funnel needs
- A confirmation email the moment someone opts in or buys.
- A nurture or onboarding sequence for the first week.
- Tags or pipeline stages, so you can see where each person is.
- A follow-up for people who start checkout and leave.
- Reminders for any live event or call.

## Is the offer ready for a funnel
An offer is ready when you can say who it is for and what result they get in one sentence, a few people have bought it or asked for it, and you have some proof. A funnel scales what already sells in conversations. If nobody has bought yet, sell it by hand first.
