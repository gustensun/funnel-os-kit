---
title: The 17Ps of funnel sections
---
The 17Ps is the order of sections on a sales page or any page that asks for a decision. Use only the sections the page needs. An opt-in page might use six of them, and a long sales page can use all seventeen. Every section you leave out should be a decision.

## The seventeen sections, in order
1. Promise: the outcome they want, in plain words, in the headline.
2. Problem: what makes them want a change now.
3. Pain Points: the symptoms they recognise from their own week.
4. Proof of Results: why you are a credible guide, with real results.
5. Personal Epiphany: how you found this way of doing it.
6. Process to Solution: the method that gets the result, in a few named steps.
7. Positioning of Offer: why this beats the other options they are weighing.
8. Price Anchor: the value shown next to the price, so the deal is easy to see.
9. Pricing Tables: only when there are several plans to choose from.
10. Product Breakdown: what is inside, item by item.
11. Product Bonuses: what else they get. Each bonus removes one reason not to buy.
12. Proof of Recommendations: reviews, testimonials and outside trust.
13. Perfect Persona: who it is for and who should skip it.
14. Project Spotlight: one or two case studies told in detail.
15. Pushback Reflex: the guarantee and the risk reversal.
16. Priority: why act now, with a real deadline or limit.
17. Promotional Bar: the announcement bar at the top and the final call to action at the bottom.

An FAQ can follow Priority when objections remain.

## Default sections by page type
- Opt-in or lead page: Promotional Bar, Promise, Pain Points, Product Breakdown (what is in the freebie), Proof of Results and the call to action.
- Webinar or challenge registration: Promotional Bar, Promise, Pain Points, Product Breakdown (what they will learn), Proof of Results and Priority (the date).
- Low-ticket page ($7 to $47): Promotional Bar, Promise, Proof of Results, Personal Epiphany, Positioning (before and after), Product Breakdown, Product Bonuses, Price Anchor, Proof of Recommendations, Pushback Reflex, FAQ and Priority.
- Automated or mid-ticket sales page ($100 to $1,000): all seventeen, with Pricing Tables only when there are plans.
- High-ticket or application page: Promise, Problem, Pain Points, Proof of Results, Personal Epiphany, Process to Solution, Perfect Persona, Project Spotlight, Proof of Recommendations and Priority, with an application as the call to action. Usually no price on the page.
- Upsell or one-time offer page: Promise, Product Breakdown, Price Anchor, Pushback Reflex and Priority.

## The 10P outline
The 10P outline is the compact version for pages that need every conversion section without a wall of text: Promise, Problem, Pain, Proof, Process, Positioning, Price, Product, Pushback and Priority. Keep all ten and make each one scannable with a short heading, two or three lines and a visual.

## How long a page should be
As long as it takes to answer every question and objection the buyer has. Higher prices and colder traffic need more copy, because people need more belief before they buy. A warm audience buying a low-ticket product needs much less. Long copy works when it is easy to read: short headlines, bullets, proof blocks, visuals and a call to action between sections, so people can buy the moment they are ready.
